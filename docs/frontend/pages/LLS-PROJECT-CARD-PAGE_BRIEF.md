## Primary task

Understand the LLS learning workflow and explore how its connected features fit together, while recognizing that the project is in development.

## Information hierarchy

1. Establish LLS as an in-development study workspace.
2. Use an Obsidian-inspired network graph as the visual and navigational anchor, with Brain at its center.
3. Let graph selections change the adjacent summary panel in place without scrolling or moving the graph.
4. Keep complete readable sections available in a secondary disclosure and without JavaScript.
5. Provide a return link to the current portfolio.

## Content boundaries

- Describe LLS as a personal, local-first study workspace.
- Only name features supported in the current project summary: course materials and source links, course maps, lessons, practice, progress evidence, and optional Obsidian export.
- Keep “in development” visible; do not imply a public release or a hosted multi-user service.
- Explain that AI work uses the learner’s authenticated Codex installation and that progress indicators are not calibrated mastery estimates.
- Explain that the app is the workspace and Obsidian is an optional, one-way export destination; do not say Obsidian hosts LLS.
- Use an Obsidian-inspired dark network of linked note-like dots with a few labeled hubs; do not imply this is a screenshot of a real Obsidian vault.

## Visual and interaction direction

- Reuse the portfolio’s ivory, charcoal, burnt-orange, Manrope, and DM Sans system.
- Use semantic links for graph selection and anchor fallback, visible focus, and synchronized selected states.
- Keep the graph and detail panel together in the primary view; all six long-form sections remain available in a disclosure.
- Keep the page responsive without hover-dependent content.
- Honor reduced-motion preferences and preserve a conventional section index as a recovery path.
- Keep this route out of public homepage navigation until approved.
